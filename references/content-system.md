# Content and Page Production System

Use this reference whenever the task creates, rewrites, briefs, designs or approves a public page, landing page, blog post, guide, comparison, review, category, product page, local page, research asset or other indexable content.

## 1. Definition of Quality

Length is an outcome of solving the user's job with enough evidence; it is not a quality target or an SEO factor.

- Never set a universal minimum such as 1,000, 1,500 or 2,000 words.
- Never copy the average length of ranking pages as a publication requirement.
- A short definition or utility page can be complete. Do not pad it.
- A 700-word article fails when it omits decisions, proof, examples, limitations, media, product utility or next steps required by its purpose.
- A 5,000-word article fails when it repeats consensus, hides the answer or adds sections only to cover keywords.
- If the user requests a long-form asset, use the requested range as a production constraint, but still remove filler and flag when the topic cannot support the range honestly.

Treat these as the quality units:

- task completion;
- coverage of material questions and decisions;
- accuracy and claim support;
- original or first-hand contribution;
- specificity, examples and edge cases;
- useful media, data or functionality;
- clear information architecture;
- trust, provenance and limitations;
- an honest next action;
- accessibility, performance and lifecycle.

This is an operational editorial rubric, not a claim about direct ranking factors.

## 2. Page Admission Gate

Before creating a new indexable URL, establish:

- `User and circumstance`: who needs it and in what situation.
- `Job`: what progress, decision or action the user needs to complete.
- `Evidence of need`: queries, live SERPs, internal search, sales/support input, research, analytics or a declared hypothesis.
- `Existing alternative`: current URL, product flow, competitor resource or other way the need is served.
- `URL decision`: create, update, consolidate, redirect, noindex or do nothing.
- `Format decision`: article, landing, product/category, comparison, tool, calculator, template, video, documentation, hub or another format.
- `Acceptance criteria`: observable conditions that mean the user can finish the job.
- `Index decision`: why this version deserves a searchable canonical URL.

Fail admission when the only rationale is a keyword, a fan-out query or the ability to generate the page. Prefer one strong canonical resource when query variants share the same user job.

## 3. Content Contract

Do not draft a complete or definitive page until the contract below is resolved or explicitly marked as missing data.

### Context and outcome

- Business goal and audience.
- Market, language, device and stage of awareness.
- Primary user job and desired progress.
- Primary page action and what happens after it.
- Page family and canonical URL.
- Existing pages reviewed and cannibalization decision.
- Baseline and success metric when available.

### Intent and coverage

- Primary intent and secondary intents that belong on the same page.
- Questions the reader must be able to answer.
- Decisions the reader must be able to make.
- Prerequisites and vocabulary.
- Objections, failure modes, constraints and edge cases.
- Explicit exclusions so the page does not expand indefinitely.
- Relevant entities and relationships.

### Contribution and evidence

- What the current SERP or source set already says.
- What is missing, weak, contradictory or difficult to use.
- The page's real contribution: data, experience, case, expert view, tested comparison, original media, tool, template, workflow, synthesis or decision framework.
- Source packet with primary sources first.
- Claim ledger with source, date, scope and limitation.
- Author, reviewer and method where readers would expect them.
- Facts or assets still missing; never fill them with invented material.

Every material number, range, threshold, comparison or safety recommendation needs an inspectable source, calculation or documented professional method. Label professional judgment and identify the accountable reviewer before presenting it publicly as a recommendation. A repeated figure across reseller pages is not validation.

### Experience and design

- Information order based on user priority.
- First-screen answer, promise or decision context.
- Required components: prose, steps, cards, table, calculator, form, gallery, diagram, video or download.
- Proof placement beside the relevant claim.
- Mobile and extreme-content behavior.
- Loading, empty, error, success, disabled and permission states for interactive components.
- Media purpose, source, rights, caption, alt behavior and performance constraints.
- Accessibility and agent-readability requirements.

### Growth and lifecycle

- Natural product contribution, if any.
- CTA matched to intent: self-serve, assisted sale, contact, download, next article or none.
- Incoming and outgoing internal links.
- External distribution and repurposable assets.
- Tracking events and guardrail metrics.
- Owner, review date, update triggers and consolidation/removal criteria.

## 4. Depth and Completeness Gate

Build a coverage matrix before drafting:

| Requirement | Why it matters | Evidence or asset | Planned section/component | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| User decision/question | | | | Required / ready / missing / excluded |

The piece is complete only when:

- every required question and decision has a useful answer;
- material claims are supported, qualified or removed;
- the user can complete the defined job without a predictable second search;
- the original contribution is visible and material;
- examples, exceptions and limitations exist where the decision needs them;
- media or functionality required by the job works, rather than being described only in copy;
- the next action is clear and honest;
- no section exists solely to increase length or repeat a keyword;
- missing evidence is exposed and blocks publication when material.

After drafting, perform:

1. `Reverse outline`: write the distinct contribution of every section in one sentence. Merge or remove duplicates.
2. `Second-search test`: list what a serious reader would still need to search for. Resolve material gaps.
3. `Specificity test`: replace unsupported generalities with a mechanism, instruction, example, datum or honest limitation.
4. `Deletion test`: remove text whose deletion does not change understanding, confidence, decision or action.
5. `Coverage reconciliation`: map every contract requirement to the final artifact.

Do not stop because each heading has one paragraph. Stop when the contract passes without filler.

## 5. Evidence and Originality

Use an information-delta ledger:

| Question or claim | Existing consensus | Gap or weakness | Our contribution | Evidence | Section |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Valid contributions include:

- first-party data with a stated method;
- first-hand use, test, visit or workflow evidence;
- a real case with context and limitations;
- an identified expert's accountable judgment;
- a comparison using the same criteria and conditions;
- an executable tool, template, dataset or process;
- original screenshots, images, diagrams or demonstrations;
- a synthesis that resolves a decision existing sources leave unresolved;
- counterexamples and limitations that change the recommendation.

If the draft only paraphrases available consensus, it fails even when polished. Do not call `information gain` a confirmed ranking factor; use it as an editorial shorthand for material added value.

For original studies, benchmarks, surveys or rankings, require:

- question and hypothesis;
- data owner, source and collection period;
- population, sample and inclusion/exclusion rules;
- metric definitions and transformations;
- method, limitations, bias and date cut-off;
- reviewer and correction policy;
- separation of observation, calculation and interpretation;
- downloadable or inspectable data when viable.

Never publish a proprietary percentage, score or ranking without a reproducible method.

For AI-assisted production:

- verify every material fact against the original source;
- inspect links and confirm the cited page supports the sentence;
- review metadata, alt text and structured data as carefully as body copy;
- disclose automation when readers would reasonably expect to know how the work was produced;
- preserve asset provenance and required AI-media metadata;
- require expert review proportional to YMYL, legal, safety or financial risk.
- do not set `datePublished`, `dateModified`, “last reviewed” or a current-looking freshness date unless that event really occurred for the final public artifact under an identified process.

## 6. Page-Family Contracts

Adapt the contract; do not force one outline onto every page.

| Page family | Required decision units | Required differentiation | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Definition or glossary | Direct definition, boundaries, example, related/confused concepts, source | Clearer synthesis or domain-specific example | Inflated into a generic article |
| How-to or tutorial | Outcome, prerequisites, steps, decision points, example, errors, edge cases, verification | Tested workflow, screenshots, code, template or practitioner experience | Steps without proof or success check |
| Editorial analysis | Thesis, evidence, counterargument, implications, action | Reporting, expert view, original synthesis or data | Summary of other articles |
| Service landing | Audience, problem, mechanism, scope, process, proof, objections, limits, CTA | Real cases, method, team, deliverables and constraints | Generic promises and stock proof |
| SaaS use case | Job, workflow, product screens, integration, time to value, limits, activation | Product demonstration and real scenario | Feature list with forced CTA |
| Comparison or alternatives | User context, candidate set, common criteria, method, tradeoffs, fit/no-fit, verdicts | Homogeneous testing/data and transparent methodology | Invented competitor claims or one hidden winner |
| Review or ranking | Scope, tested set, method, evidence, measurements, pros/cons, who it fits, sellers/options | First-hand evidence or clearly labeled documentary analysis | A thin summary presented as hands-on testing |
| Product or ecommerce | Decision attributes, variants, price/stock/shipping/returns when real, media, support, proof, CTA | Accurate first-party data, demonstration, real reviews | Manufacturer copy or stale offers |
| Category or hub | Selection model, inventory, filters, comparison help, subtopics, navigation | Useful curation, data and pathways | A paragraph above a grid |
| Local page | Real service capacity, coverage, local proof, team/process, contact, limitations | Verifiable local evidence and differentiated proposition | City-name substitutions or doorway pages |
| Research or data asset | Method, dataset, findings, visualization, uncertainty, implications, download | Original data and reproducibility | Statistics without denominator or method |
| Entity, brand or author | Identity, role, credentials, relationships, authoritative references, current facts | Verifiable facts and provenance | Inflated credentials or keyword biography |
| Tool, calculator or template | Input, result, method, examples, errors, privacy, accessibility, next step | Working utility and trustworthy calculation | Long copy around a weak or fake tool |

For programmatic families, also load [programmatic-seo.md](programmatic-seo.md).

## 7. Content Design, Media and Accessibility

Design the page, not only the prose.

- Put the main answer, value or decision context early without hiding necessary nuance.
- Order sections by user need, not keyword prominence.
- Use descriptive headings with a logical semantic hierarchy.
- Add a table of contents only when length, complexity or non-linear navigation justifies it.
- Use tables for real comparisons, lists for processes and prose for explanation; do not add formats decoratively.
- Use bold emphasis sparingly for scanability, never as keyword decoration.
- Place proof and sources beside the claims they support.
- Keep critical answers, price, limitations and proof visible; use accordions only for optional detail.
- Give interactive controls semantic elements, accessible names, visible focus and clear state changes.
- Keep the mobile page equivalent in content and task completion.

Every media asset must have a declared job: explain, demonstrate, compare, orient, prove, reduce uncertainty or enable action. Record:

- source, creator, rights and date;
- caption and surrounding context;
- informative, functional, complex or decorative alt treatment;
- responsive dimensions and performance budget;
- transcript, captions, audio description or data equivalent when required;
- relationship to structured data and social metadata.

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the default product baseline unless project or legal requirements are stronger. Validate applicable items including title, landmarks, headings, reading/focus order, link purpose, keyboard operation, focus visibility, contrast, resize/reflow, target size, labels, errors, media alternatives and reduced motion. Automated scans do not prove conformance; add manual keyboard, zoom/reflow and assistive-technology smoke checks.

When agent journeys matter, inspect the rendered page, DOM and accessibility tree. Prefer stable layouts, semantic links/buttons, associated labels and explicit success/error states. Treat emerging agent protocols as optional and versioned, not as substitutes for a usable website.

## 8. Drafting and Revision Workflow

1. Admit or reject the page.
2. Inspect existing assets, URLs, metrics and product truth.
3. Observe current SERPs and query variants for intent and format, not for content cloning.
4. Build the source packet, claim ledger, information delta and coverage matrix.
5. Approve the content, design, product and measurement contract.
6. Draft from the contract; do not draft from a keyword list alone.
7. Fact-check and reconcile claims with sources.
8. Run reverse-outline, second-search, specificity and deletion tests.
9. Review product value, CTA, accessibility, mobile, media, links and metadata.
10. Render and validate the final artifact when code or a URL exists.
11. Keep the artifact in draft/review state if material evidence, rights, expert approval or functionality is missing.

## 9. Publication Definition of Done

Use precise readiness states:

- `brief-ready`: strategy/requirements are complete; public copy is not.
- `copy-complete`: the prose/components specification is complete, but implementation or publication inputs may remain.
- `implementation-pending`: code/CMS integration, real metadata, assets, links, tracking or rendered QA remains.
- `evidence-blocked`: a material fact, test, method, expert review, right or product truth is missing.
- `review-approved`: accountable editorial/legal/expert reviewers have approved their scopes.
- `publish-ready`: every required evidence, implementation, review and QA gate has actually passed.

Never describe a body as publishable “as is” while any material dependency remains. State exactly what is complete and what still blocks publication.

A public page is ready only when:

- admission and canonical decisions are recorded;
- every required contract item maps to final content or a working component;
- claims, comparisons, prices, availability, reviews and credentials are real and current;
- material numeric recommendations have a source, calculation or accountable method;
- original value is visible;
- the main user job can be completed;
- content contains neither material omission nor filler;
- metadata, schema, visible content and media agree;
- design, mobile, accessibility and critical states were reviewed;
- internal links and distribution have owners;
- analytics distinguish discovery, task success, activation and business outcomes;
- lifecycle ownership and update triggers exist;
- all gates in [qa-gates.md](qa-gates.md) pass.
