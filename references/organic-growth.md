Use this reference for organic-growth strategy, content portfolios, conversion, SaaS, ecommerce, tools/templates, product-led content, digital PR, lifecycle work or any page whose success must extend beyond visibility.

## 1. Growth Model

Organic traffic is an input, not the outcome. Model the complete path:

`eligible → shown/cited → visited → task completed → activated → converted → retained/expanded → advocated or contributed`

Do not imply that every project is product-led. First classify the motion:

- self-serve product-led;
- sales-assisted product-led;
- sales-led or service-led;
- ecommerce/transactional;
- publisher/audience;
- local lead generation;
- marketplace or community.

Use a funnel to define a page's local sequence and friction. Use a loop only when one user's or cohort's output can feed the next acquisition or engagement cycle. Do not invent a loop for a one-way acquisition page.

For every important page or initiative, state:

- journey role: discovery, evaluation, decision, activation, support, retention or expansion;
- next best action;
- first meaningful value event;
- business outcome;
- reinvestable loop output, if one exists;
- cycle time and guardrails.

## 2. Strategy Inputs

Establish before prioritizing:

- business model, offer, margin and capacity;
- ideal customer or audience and jobs-to-be-done;
- markets, languages, devices and seasonality;
- product truth: features, inventory, data, integrations, proof and constraints;
- conversion and activation definitions based on real behavior;
- existing demand, branded demand and category maturity;
- current organic performance by landing-page family and intent;
- distribution assets, authority, community and partnerships;
- editorial, design, engineering, legal and expert capacity;
- acceptable risk, time horizon and measurement limitations.

Avoid traffic-volume prioritization alone. Score opportunities using evidence and explain the reasoning across:

- user/business value;
- product or offer fit;
- demand and intent confidence;
- ability to add differentiated value;
- authority and distribution feasibility;
- conversion/activation path;
- effort, dependencies, maintenance and risk;
- time to leading signal and business outcome.

Do not hide weak evidence behind a single composite score.

## 3. Opportunity Portfolio

Build a balanced portfolio rather than a blog calendar detached from the product.

| Motion | Best use | Required value | Typical business link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand capture | Existing category, product, service, comparison or local intent | Better decision support and offer fit | Lead, sale, signup, visit |
| Expert education | Complex problems and trust building | Experience, method, examples, judgment | Assisted conversion, authority |
| Product-led content | Product can materially complete the job | Demonstration, workflow and time to value | Activation and conversion |
| Free tool/calculator/template | Repeated task with executable value | Working utility, method and privacy clarity | Trial, lead, sharing, retention |
| Integration/use-case pages | Real supported workflows | Accurate integration and product steps | Activation, ecosystem demand |
| Original research/data | Proprietary or defensible evidence | Transparent method and reusable assets | Links, mentions, sales enablement |
| Programmatic/data pages | Repeated jobs served by unique data/utility | Reliable dataset and page-worthiness | Scalable acquisition and product use |
| Community/UGC | Users create valuable, moderatable knowledge | Quality controls, privacy and incentives | Retention, advocacy, long-tail demand |
| Digital PR/brand authority | A newsworthy finding, tool or expert view | Evidence and journalist/audience relevance | Earned mentions, links, branded demand |
| Lifecycle/support content | Activation, retention or expansion friction | Contextual help tied to product behavior | Lower support, higher retention |

For each portfolio item, connect the search opportunity to the product or business mechanism. Do not force product mentions where they do not help the user.

## 4. Product-Led Content Contract

For every topic, answer:

- Can the product or service genuinely help complete the job?
- At which exact step?
- What visual, functional or first-party evidence proves it?
- What limitation, prerequisite or non-fit must be disclosed?
- What action lets the user experience value?
- Is self-serve, assisted sale, purchase, contact or no commercial CTA appropriate?

Use this qualitative business-potential rubric only for prioritization, not as a ranking factor:

- `3`: the product is essential to completing the job.
- `2`: the product materially improves the outcome.
- `1`: the connection is indirect but useful and honest.
- `0`: do not force the product; justify editorial, audience or authority value separately.

Product-led pages must demonstrate, not merely claim:

- real product screens, steps, outputs, data or workflows;
- time to first value and next activation event;
- limitations and fit/no-fit conditions;
- what happens after the CTA;
- a fallback for users who are not ready to convert.

Do not disguise a feature list as educational content. Do not withhold the promised answer to force signup.

## 5. Journey and Conversion Design

For each page, map:

| Stage | User question | Evidence needed | Friction | Page/component response | Event |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Page-experience requirements:

- one clear primary job and primary action per context;
- answer/value proposition and decision context early;
- proof beside the claim or CTA it supports;
- CTA language that matches the destination and commitment;
- secondary actions that do not compete visually with the primary action;
- forms that ask only for information necessary at that stage;
- visible price, limitations, requirements or next steps when they affect the decision;
- loading, empty, error, success and recovery states for product components;
- responsive layout, readable hierarchy and accessible interaction;
- performance budgets aligned with real-user experience.

For high-consideration or sales-led offers, the correct conversion may be a diagnostic, case, calculator or qualified conversation rather than signup. For informational intent, the correct next action may be another resource or no commercial CTA.

Never claim CRO improvement without controlled evidence. Treat best practices as hypotheses and test material changes.

## 6. Distribution and Authority

Publishing is not distribution. Every priority URL needs:

- at least one relevant, crawlable incoming internal link;
- a place in hubs, navigation, related modules or sitemaps appropriate to its value;
- a launch and resurfacing plan for the audience: email, community, social, partner, PR, sales, support or customer success;
- channel-specific assets and messaging;
- an owner, date and tracking method;
- update and redistribution triggers.

Earned authority should come from useful assets, expertise, relationships and real brand demand. Do not create fake mentions, reviews, citations, profiles or UGC.

For research/digital PR, define:

- the audience and newsworthy claim;
- original evidence and method;
- source files, visuals and expert availability;
- target publications/communities and relevance;
- disclosure, corrections and conflict-of-interest policy;
- links/mentions as earned outcomes, never guaranteed deliverables.

## 7. Measurement and Experiments

Create a measurement contract:

| Layer | Question | Metric | Source | Segment | Baseline | Success/stop condition | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Separate:

- `Eligibility`: crawlability, indexability, indexation and valid feeds/schema.
- `Visibility`: impressions, rankings, cited pages, citations, mentions and share of visibility.
- `Acquisition`: qualified clicks, landing users and referral sources.
- `Task success`: tool use, completion, downloads, helpfulness, support deflection or search refinement.
- `Activation`: the product-specific first value event for organic cohorts.
- `Conversion`: qualified lead, trial, demo, purchase, revenue or assisted conversion.
- `Retention/expansion`: return use, cohort retention, upgrades or repeated purchases.
- `Loop`: output generated per activated user and loop cycle time.
- `Guardrails`: errors, abandonment, complaints, accessibility, CWV, low-quality leads and returns.

Scroll depth, time on page and raw sessions are diagnostic, not universal success metrics or ranking factors.

For an experiment, record:

- hypothesis and causal mechanism;
- unit and segment;
- baseline and comparison/control;
- change and affected pages;
- primary, leading and guardrail metrics;
- minimum observation window and seasonality;
- confounders and concurrent changes;
- success, stop and rollback criteria;
- result as observed, inferred or inconclusive.

AI-answer visibility is volatile. Keep engines and surfaces separate, repeat prompts/queries, record date/market/device/model or interface when known, and do not infer causality from a screenshot or aggregate visibility score.

## 8. Operating Roadmap

Deliver strategy as executable initiatives:

| Priority | Initiative | User/business problem | Evidence | Pages/product surface | Owner | Dependencies | Leading indicator | Business metric | Risk | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Sequence work in this order when dependencies require it:

1. Fix eligibility, measurement or severe trust failures.
2. Improve existing high-value pages and journeys.
3. Build missing product/content experiences with proven need.
4. Establish distribution and authority mechanisms.
5. Pilot scalable/programmatic systems.
6. Scale only after page, template, cohort and business-quality gates pass.
7. Refresh, consolidate or remove assets based on lifecycle evidence.

Do not forecast traffic, citations or revenue with false precision. State assumptions, ranges, dependencies and the signal that will update the decision.
